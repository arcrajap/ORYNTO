# Product Thesis

## What ORYNTO is

ORYNTO is an **AI Business Operator** for small and mid-sized businesses.

The product starts from a simple observation: real business activity is scattered across conversations, voice notes, email, documents, payments, spreadsheets, and human memory. Traditional business software asks people to manually translate that reality into modules and forms.

ORYNTO should reverse that relationship.

The system should understand business activity where it naturally occurs, connect it to durable business context, propose or perform authorized actions, and keep deterministic systems of record correct underneath.

## Core user promise

**Your business already talks. Now it can think and act.**

A user should be able to tell, forward, photograph, email, paste, upload, or ask—and ORYNTO should convert that input into grounded business understanding and safe action.

## Product model

ORYNTO is built around five capabilities, not five disconnected modules:

1. **See** — ingest and normalize conversations, documents, payments, sheets, voice, and operational events.
2. **Remember** — maintain durable context about people, companies, documents, commitments, history, and relationships.
3. **Understand** — interpret intent, entities, discrepancies, risk, state, and likely next action.
4. **Act** — invoke trusted business capabilities such as invoices, bills, payments, reminders, stock actions, and communication.
5. **Prove** — preserve deterministic books, audit history, authorization, citations, and traceability.

## What ORYNTO is not

ORYNTO is not:

- a chatbot wrapped around accounting software;
- a new ERP module wall;
- a replacement for deterministic accounting logic with LLM reasoning;
- a CRM/helpdesk disguised as accounting;
- an n8n-style workflow canvas for every tenant;
- a second ledger living inside chat history;
- a WhatsApp automation hack using unofficial APIs;
- a collection of channel-specific bots with duplicated business rules.

## Strategic wedge

The initial commercial wedge may be accounting-heavy because accounting provides unusually strong business truth and BigCapital can accelerate the deterministic core. But the enduring product is broader: ORYNTO should become the operating intelligence that connects customer, supplier, operational, financial, and communication context.

Accounting is a foundational truth system, not the product ceiling.

## Target experience

A business owner should be able to say:

> Send Kumar the same 40 boxes as last month. Price unchanged.

ORYNTO should identify the customer, retrieve the relevant prior order, check current business state, prepare the correct document/action, show what it inferred, and require the appropriate confirmation before external or financial commitment.

A supplier can email a PDF invoice. ORYNTO should connect it to the supplier and purchase context, surface discrepancies, and prepare a grounded draft rather than treating the PDF as an isolated OCR task.

A customer can write that they paid part of an invoice and will pay the balance next week. ORYNTO should preserve the communication, associate it with the correct business relationship and outstanding documents, and create useful follow-up context without silently changing the ledger.

## User generations

ORYNTO must support both:

- **conversation-first / AI-first users**, who expect intent-driven software;
- **classic/professional users**, including accountants and operations staff who need precise deterministic screens, reports, exports, and audit paths.

These are two interfaces to one business truth, not two products.

## Global architecture, local packs

The architecture is global from the start. Country-specific tax, payments, banking, invoice rules, identity, language, and compliance behavior should be implemented as country capabilities/packs rather than embedded assumptions in the core.

India is the preferred first country pack unless a later recorded decision changes sequencing.

## Success condition

ORYNTO wins when a business can operate naturally through its existing communication and work habits while becoming **more structured, more auditable, and less dependent on manual back-office translation**.

The long-term ambition is that a small company should possess operational intelligence previously available only to organizations with dedicated finance, operations, CRM, and back-office teams.
